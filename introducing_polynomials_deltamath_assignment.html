🎯 Learning Targets

By the end of this activity, I can:

  • Identify the least possible degree of a polynomial from its graph.
  • Determine whether the leading coefficient is positive or negative.
  • Count different real zeros (distinct x-intercepts, including touches).
  • Count relative extrema (local maxima and minima).
  • Use end behavior to explain my reasoning.
📌 Important: The shape of a graph can suggest the least possible degree, but it usually does not prove the exact degree without additional information.

🧠 Quick Review: Four Graph Clues

1️⃣ Degree

A degree-n polynomial has at most n − 1 turning points. So 4 turning points require degree at least 5.

2️⃣ Leading Coefficient

Look at the right end: if it rises, the leading coefficient is positive; if it falls, it is negative.

3️⃣ Different Real Zeros

Count the distinct x-values where the graph crosses or touches the x-axis. A touch counts once.

4️⃣ Relative Extrema

Count local peaks ⛰️ and local valleys 🏞️. Each is one relative extremum.

🔍 End behavior: Opposite ends mean odd degree; matching ends mean even degree. Combine this with turning points to find the least possible degree.

👨‍🏫 I DO — Let's Read a Graph

Study the original example graph below. It represents f(x) = x³ − 3x.

1️⃣ End behavior: Left end falls; right end rises → odd degree and positive leading coefficient.
2️⃣ Relative extrema: One peak and one valley → 2 extrema.
3️⃣ Zeros: The graph crosses the x-axis at 3 different points.
4️⃣ Least possible degree: 2 turning points require degree at least 3; the ends indicate odd degree → 3.

🎉 Answer: Least possible degree 3; leading coefficient positive; 3 distinct real zeros; 2 relative extrema.
