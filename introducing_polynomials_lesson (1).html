📖 KEY VOCABULARY

Polynomial: A finite sum of terms with real-number coefficients and nonnegative integer exponents of x.

Degree: The greatest exponent after simplifying (for nonzero polynomials).

Leading coefficient: Coefficient of the highest-degree term.

Constant term: Term without a variable.

Zero: An x-value making f(x) = 0.

x-intercept: Point where a graph touches or crosses the x-axis.

y-intercept: Point where x = 0.

Turning point: A point where a graph changes between increasing and decreasing.

End behavior: What happens to f(x) as x goes far left or right.

👨‍🏫 5. I DO — TEACHER MODELING

🧮 Part A: What Is a Polynomial?

f(x) = aₙxⁿ + aₙ₋₁xⁿ⁻¹ + … + a₁x + a₀

The coefficients are real numbers; exponents are nonnegative integers; and the leading coefficient of a nonzero polynomial is not zero.

✅ Examples: 3x² + 5x − 7; −2x³ + 4x; 5x⁴ − x² + 8; 9.

❌ Nonexamples: 3/x + 2; √x + 4; x⁻² + 5.

🔎 Part B: Degree & Leading Coefficient

f(x) = 4x⁵ − 3x³ + 2x − 8

Degree = 5; leading coefficient = 4; constant = −8; classification = quintic.

g(x) = −2x⁴ + 7x² − 1

Degree = 4; leading coefficient = −2; constant = −1; classification = quartic.

💡 Write in descending exponent order before identifying the leading coefficient!

📈 Part C: End Behavior

DegreeLeading CoefficientLeft EndRight End
EvenPositive⬆️ Up⬆️ Up
EvenNegative⬇️ Down⬇️ Down
OddPositive⬇️ Down⬆️ Up
OddNegative⬆️ Up⬇️ Down

🎨 Visualize the Four End-Behavior Families

Even + PositiveBoth ends up
Even + NegativeBoth ends down
Odd + PositiveLeft down, right up
Odd + NegativeLeft up, right down

Example: f(x) = −3x³ + 2x² + 5 has odd degree (3) and negative leading coefficient (−3), so it rises on the left and falls on the right.

👀 Reveal Solution

🧩 Example 2: g(x) = −x⁴ + 3x² − 2

Identify degree, leading coefficient, constant, end behavior, and y-intercept.

👀 Reveal Solution
Degree 4; leading coefficient −1; constant −2; both ends down; y-intercept (0, −2).

🧩 Example 3: h(x) = (x − 1)(x + 2)(x − 3)

Find degree, leading coefficient, zeros, intercepts, and end behavior.

👀 Reveal Solution
Degree 3; leading coefficient 1; zeros 1, −2, 3; x-intercepts (1, 0), (−2, 0), (3, 0); y-intercept (0, 6); left down, right up.

🎯 Problem 1

🎯 Problem 2

🎯 Problem 3

h(x) = (x + 1)(x − 2). Determine zeros, intercepts, degree, and end behavior. Sketch the graph.

🔓 Check Answer
Zeros −1 and 2; x-intercepts (−1, 0), (2, 0); y-intercept (0, −2); degree 2; both ends up.

🎯 Problem 4

p(x) = x³ − 4x. Find zeros and end behavior. Sketch the graph.

🔓 Check Answer

🎯 Problem 5

Describe the end behavior of an even-degree polynomial with a negative leading coefficient.

🔓 Check Answer
Both ends down. Example: y = −x².

🚨 11. COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS & RETEACHING

❌ Degree means the number of terms. ✅ Degree is the greatest exponent after simplifying.

❌ Leading coefficient is always the first number written. ✅ Put the polynomial in standard form.

❌ Every polynomial has the same graph shape. ✅ Degree and coefficients affect the shape.

❌ Every x-intercept is a turning point. ✅ Intercepts and turning points describe different features.

❌ Degree gives the exact number of turning points. ✅ A degree-n polynomial has at most n − 1 turning points.
