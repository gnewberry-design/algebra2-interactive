📖 KEY VOCABULARY

🔹 Polynomial: An expression made of terms with real-number coefficients and nonnegative integer variable exponents.

🔹 Degree: The greatest exponent after simplifying.

🔹 Leading Coefficient: The coefficient of the highest-degree term.

🔹 Constant Term: A term without a variable.

🔹 Zero: An x-value that makes f(x) = 0.

🔹 x-Intercept: A point where the graph crosses or touches the x-axis.

🔹 y-Intercept: The point where the graph meets the y-axis.

🔹 Turning Point: A point where the graph changes from increasing to decreasing or vice versa.

🔹 End Behavior: How the graph behaves far to the left and right.

👀 Reveal Answer

👨‍🏫 5. I DO — TEACHER MODELING

🧮 Part A: What Is a Polynomial?

A polynomial function can be written as:

f(x) = aₙxⁿ + aₙ₋₁xⁿ⁻¹ + … + a₁x + a₀

The coefficients are real numbers, the exponents are nonnegative integers, and the leading coefficient is not zero.

✅ Examples: 3x² + 5x − 7; −2x³ + 4x; 5x⁴ − x² + 8; 9.

❌ Nonexamples: 3/x + 2; √x + 4; x⁻² + 5.

🔎 Part B: Degree & Leading Coefficient

Example 1: f(x) = 4x⁵ − 3x³ + 2x − 8

🎯 Degree = 5

⭐ Leading Coefficient = 4

🔢 Constant = −8

📚 Classification = Quintic

Example 2: g(x) = −2x⁴ + 7x² − 1

🎯 Degree = 4

⭐ Leading Coefficient = −2

🔢 Constant = −1

📚 Classification = Quartic

💡 Tip: Write the polynomial in descending exponent order first!

📈 Part C: End Behavior

The degree and leading coefficient help predict the directions of the graph's ends.

DegreeLeading CoefficientLeft EndRight End
EvenPositive⬆️ Up⬆️ Up
EvenNegative⬇️ Down⬇️ Down
OddPositive⬇️ Down⬆️ Up
OddNegative⬆️ Up⬇️ Down

🎨 Four Common End-Behavior Shapes

🟢 Even Degree + Positive Leading Coefficient: Both ends UP.

🔴 Even Degree + Negative Leading Coefficient: Both ends DOWN.

🔵 Odd Degree + Positive Leading Coefficient: Left DOWN, right UP.

🟣 Odd Degree + Negative Leading Coefficient: Left UP, right DOWN.

Teacher Example: f(x) = −3x³ + 2x² + 5

Degree = 3 (odd), leading coefficient = −3 (negative).

Therefore: Left end UP; right end DOWN.

📍 Part D: Key Graphical Features

  • 🎯 x-Intercepts: Where the graph crosses or touches the x-axis.
  • 📌 y-Intercept: Where x = 0.
  • 🔢 Zeros: Values of x for which f(x) = 0.
  • 🔄 Turning Points: Where a graph changes direction.
  • ↔️ End Behavior: What happens far left and far right.

Example: f(x) = x² − 4

Degree = 2; leading coefficient = 1.

Zeros = −2 and 2.

x-intercepts = (−2, 0) and (2, 0).

y-intercept = (0, −4).

Turning point = (0, −4).

End behavior = Both ends UP.

🤝 6. WE DO — GUIDED PRACTICE

Students respond verbally or in Teams chat before revealing each solution.

🧩 Example 1

f(x) = 2x³ − 5x + 1

Identify the degree, leading coefficient, constant, end behavior, and y-intercept.

👀 Reveal Solution

Degree = 3; leading coefficient = 2; constant = 1.

End behavior = Left DOWN, right UP.

y-intercept = (0, 1).

🧩 Example 2

g(x) = −x⁴ + 3x² − 2

Identify the degree, leading coefficient, constant, end behavior, and y-intercept.

👀 Reveal Solution

Degree = 4; leading coefficient = −1; constant = −2.

End behavior = Both ends DOWN.

y-intercept = (0, −2).

🧩 Example 3

h(x) = (x − 1)(x + 2)(x − 3)

Determine degree, leading coefficient, zeros, intercepts, and end behavior.

👀 Reveal Solution

Degree = 3; leading coefficient = 1.

Zeros = 1, −2, 3.

x-intercepts = (1, 0), (−2, 0), (3, 0).

y-intercept = (0, 6).

End behavior = Left DOWN, right UP.

🚨 11. COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS & RETEACHING

❌ Mistake: Degree means the number of terms.

✅ Correction: Degree is the greatest exponent after simplifying.

❌ Mistake: Leading coefficient is always the first number written.

✅ Correction: Write in standard form first.

❌ Mistake: Every polynomial has the same graph shape.

✅ Correction: Degree and coefficients affect the graph.

❌ Mistake: Every x-intercept is a turning point.

✅ Correction: Intercepts and turning points describe different features.

❌ Mistake: Degree tells the exact number of turning points.

✅ Correction: Degree n allows at most n − 1 turning points.
