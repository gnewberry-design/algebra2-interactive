🌎 WHY DOES BELONGING MATTER?

Starting high school can bring new classes, friendships, expectations, and questions about where we fit in. Sometimes our brains make quick guesses about what others think of us. A guess can feel true without being the complete story.

🤝 Relationships

Belonging can grow when we reach out and include others.

📚 Learning

Feeling welcome can make it easier to participate and ask for help.

🌱 Growth

One awkward moment does not define who we are or where we belong.

Big idea: Reframing is not pretending everything is perfect. It is considering a fairer, more helpful interpretation of a situation.

📖 Key Vocabulary

Belonging: Feeling accepted, respected, and connected.

Thought: An interpretation or idea our mind creates.

Assumption: Something we believe without having all the evidence.

Reframe: Consider another realistic way to interpret a situation.

Perspective-taking: Considering how a situation might look from another point of view.

🔥 4. WARM-UP — QUICK CHECK-IN

Imagine: You say hello to a classmate, but they do not respond.

💭 Think: What are three possible explanations for why they did not respond?
👀 Reveal Possible Explanations
They may not have heard you. They may be distracted or worried. They may not know you were speaking to them. They also might not want to talk right then. We cannot know the reason from this one moment alone.

Discussion: Which explanations are facts, and which are guesses?

Students may respond in their heads, on paper, or in chat without sharing personal information.

👨‍🏫 5. I DO — TEACHER MODELING

🧠 The Thought → Feeling → Action Connection

💭 Thought

“Nobody wants me in the group.”

💗 Feeling

Hurt, nervous, or discouraged.

👣 Possible Action

Stay silent or avoid joining.

Thoughts can affect feelings and choices. We cannot control every thought that appears, but we can practice examining it.

🔍 The 4-Step Reframing Strategy

1. NOTICE: What am I telling myself?

2. CHECK: What do I actually know? What am I assuming?

3. REFRAME: What is another believable explanation?

4. ACT: What small, respectful step could I try?

🎭 Teacher Think-Aloud

Situation: “My classmates started a group chat without me.”

❌ First thought: “They all dislike me.”

🔎 Evidence check: “I know I am not in this chat. I do not know why.”

🌈 Balanced reframe: “I feel left out, and that feeling matters. There may be reasons I do not know. I can ask about joining or connect with someone another way.”

👣 Next step: “I could politely ask a classmate about the group or reach out to someone I trust.”

Important: Reframing does not mean ignoring bullying, discrimination, or repeated exclusion. Those situations deserve support from a trusted adult and appropriate school follow-up.

🤝 6. WE DO — GUIDED PRACTICE

Work through these fictional situations together. Students can suggest a reframe without sharing personal experiences.

🎒 Scenario A: The Lunch Table

A student approaches a table. Everyone is talking, and nobody immediately makes room.

Initial thought: “They definitely don't want me here.”

👀 Reveal a Balanced Reframe
“I am not sure whether they noticed me. I could ask, ‘Is this seat taken?’ or choose another welcoming place.”

💻 Scenario B: The Online Class

A student posts an idea in Teams chat, but no one replies right away.

Initial thought: “My idea was stupid.”

👀 Reveal a Balanced Reframe
“The chat may be moving quickly, or others may be thinking. One unanswered message does not prove my idea has no value.”

👥 Scenario C: The Group Project

Two classmates choose to work together before asking another student.

Initial thought: “I'll never make friends here.”

👀 Reveal a Balanced Reframe
“This one group choice does not predict my whole year. I can look for another partner and keep building connections.”
Teacher Question: How can we make room for feelings while still checking our assumptions?

🚨 12. COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS & SUPPORT

❌ “Reframing means my feelings are wrong.”
✅ Feelings are valid. Reframing examines interpretations, not whether you are allowed to feel something.

❌ “A thought must be true because it feels true.”
✅ Strong feelings do not always provide complete evidence.

❌ “I should always think positively.”
✅ Aim for balanced, believable thoughts—not forced positivity.

❌ “If I feel excluded, I should just ignore it.”
✅ Real exclusion, harassment, or bullying should be addressed with help from trusted adults.

🎟️ 13. CLOSURE & EXIT TICKET

1. What does it mean to reframe a thought?

👀 Sample Answer
Consider another realistic and helpful way to understand a situation.

2. Is “Nobody replied, so nobody likes me” a fact or an assumption?

👀 Sample Answer
An assumption. We know nobody replied, but we do not know why.

3. Write a balanced reframe for: “I made one mistake, so I don't belong here.”

👀 Sample Answer
“Everyone makes mistakes while learning. One mistake doesn't decide whether I belong.”

4. Name one small action that can build connection.

👀 Sample Answer
Greet someone, invite a classmate to join an activity, ask a friendly question, or reach out to a trusted adult.

5. What should you do if someone is repeatedly excluding or bullying you?

👀 Sample Answer
Seek support from a trusted adult or appropriate school staff member. Reframing is not a substitute for addressing harmful behavior.
