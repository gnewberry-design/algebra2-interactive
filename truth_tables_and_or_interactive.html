📚 Vocabulary & Rules

🤝 AND (Conjunction)

p ∧ q

TRUE only if BOTH p and q are true.

Think: I finished my homework AND cleaned my room. Both must happen!

🎉 OR (Disjunction)

p ∨ q

TRUE if AT LEAST ONE of p or q is true.

Think: I can choose water OR juice. In math, both can also be true!

Statement: A sentence that is either true or false. Compound statement: Two statements joined by a logical connector.

Important: In mathematical logic, OR is inclusive. If both p and q are true, p ∨ q is also true.

🧩 WE DO — Real-World Examples

Example 1: p = “I have my notebook” (TRUE). q = “I have a pencil” (FALSE).

🤝 “I have my notebook AND a pencil” is FALSE, because one part is false.

🎉 “I have my notebook OR a pencil” is TRUE, because at least one part is true.

Example 2: p = “2 + 2 = 4” (TRUE). q = “5 is even” (FALSE).

p ∧ q = FALSE   |   p ∨ q = TRUE

🚨 Common Mistakes

❌ Thinking AND is true when only one statement is true.
✅ AND needs both statements true.

❌ Thinking OR is false when both statements are true.
✅ Mathematical OR is true if one or both statements are true.

❌ Forgetting the F/F row.
✅ Both AND and OR are false when both statements are false.
